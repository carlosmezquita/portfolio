# Portfolio design

Audience: a London startup founder or engineering lead deciding whether to speak with Carlos. The page must make his experience, work and contact details easy to find.

## Visual identity

Use the identity already visible on Carlos's LinkedIn: near-black navy, cool blue, teal and the supplied blurred blue/teal artwork. The supplied artwork is the proposed header texture; the matching Portfolio image is the proposed social-preview asset. Publishing these two files was blocked by automatic approval review pending explicit user approval. The public PR uses the palette with a small CSS colour field in the meantime. The GitHub-logo variant remains a LinkedIn asset, not a website decoration.

The artwork provides continuity; it does not establish a unique identity by itself. The distinctive content is Carlos's work: a Galicia surf application, an urban-planning tool for NMarq, the Denodo automation work and rebuilding the Portsmouth AI Society. Product images must show that work.

- Background: #040C1C; text: #F1F4FA; secondary text: #A4B3CA.
- Teal #75E5D8 for identity and focus; blue #65BAFF for project links.
- Keep the visual field behind the introduction with a strong dark overlay. The body remains a solid reading surface. Substitute the supplied header artwork once its publication is approved.
- Large name, left alignment, clear section labels and ruled sections. Projects share a reading structure, not a grid of interchangeable promotional cards.
- System sans-serif; normal sentence case; no terminal typography. Mobile is a single column.
- Links remain underlined; focus is visible. No entrance effects, cursor effects, autoplay or hidden information.
- Keep the real Surfay screenshot uncropped, with its original map attribution and an explicit synthetic-data caption. The full-size image is one click away.

## Writing

Write in the first person for personal contributions. Name what the software does, the work Carlos owned and evidence a reader can inspect. Keep planned work in the future tense. Distinguish an implemented prototype from a validated product.

Remove sentences that could be pasted unchanged onto an unrelated developer's portfolio. Remove adjectives that substitute for evidence. Avoid repeating a technology list as a paragraph. Do not add sections merely to make projects equal in length.

Keep status and a relevant limitation close to the claim. Use one short note for Surfay's unvalidated forecasts. Do not fill the page with disclaimers or imply that passing engineering tests validates forecast accuracy.

## Research informing the choices

1. Tuch et al., 2012, *The role of visual complexity and prototypicality regarding first impression of websites*. Two experiments found that lower complexity and more familiar website structures improved aesthetic ratings. This concerns visual impressions, not hiring outcomes. [Author publication page](https://research.google/pubs/the-role-of-visual-complexity-and-prototypicality-regarding-first-impression-of-websites-working-towards-understanding-aesthetic-judgments/).
2. Morkes and Nielsen, 1998, *Applying Writing Guidelines to Web Pages*. A study with 21 technical users favoured concise, scannable and objective text. Its reported usability gains are specific to that experiment, not a forecast for this portfolio. [Study](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/applying-writing-guidelines-web-pages/).
3. Taste Labs, 2026, *Helping agents create things worth making*. Argues that specific brand context and checking adherence help avoid repeated generic design patterns. This is a vendor's product account, not independent experimental evidence. The useful practice here is a written visual system and comparison against Carlos's supplied assets. [Article](https://tastelabs.com/blog/helping-agents-create-things-worth-making).
4. W3C, WCAG 2.2, *Understanding Contrast (Minimum)*. Normal text requires 4.5:1 at AA; large text has a lower threshold. The palette is checked against the solid background; the artwork overlay still needs rendered verification. [Guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum).

These sources support familiar navigation, limited visual complexity, specific content and readable contrast. They do not establish an objectively unique style, detect AI authorship or demonstrate improved hiring results.

## Review before publishing

Check the top of the page and every section at desktop and 390px mobile widths. Confirm the long surname fits, the artwork does not compromise contrast, the screenshot and caption remain understandable, all links work and the CV downloads. Complete keyboard and Back-navigation checks. Ask a reviewer to identify Carlos's experience, one concrete contribution and a contact route without coaching. Treat task success separately from whether they like the colours.
